Already in the game

The header shows Range open or Range closed, the sky and the wind, then Steel, Propellant, Fleet, and Day. The version sits under those four counts and reads 0.3.0-panel. The sound button is gone. The footer is the log.

The left column is titled Land. It shows one owned total in acres and square feet. Each site still for sale is a small button with the site name, the same acre line, and the steel price. Build site is 90 steel. Pad site is 50 steel. Buying a site places its buildings. Rockets stay on the right, after you click a building.

Left column

The column is where you buy ground. It is not a shop for Starfactory, Mega Bay, or the Orbital Pad. Those arrive when the site is bought. The card should say what the purchase is, and what it costs.

Acres and square feet do not belong on that card. The acre count is the size of the parcel. Square feet repeats the same fact in a worse unit. A player deciding to spend 90 steel needs the price and one sentence about what shows up on the map.

The owned total stays above the cards, in acres only. Square feet are gone from the column. Two named rows are better than one sum once both sites are bought. That split waits until the land line on the map is settled.

This card is in index.html. A click still buys the site for the same steel cost and still places the same buildings.

Inspector layout

Starfactory’s number is the vehicle it is building, or the one it is holding. Mega Bay’s number is how many vehicles it holds, out of 4. The pad’s number is the thrust it accepts. Merlin thrust, impulse, mass, and restarts leave this card. They belong on the rocket.

Rocket

SpaceX did not raise Merlin thrust from the factory floor. Merlin 1A flew the first two Falcon 1 launches at 340 kN. The next engine, Merlin 1C, was qualified at the McGregor test site in Texas, on a stand, and then cleared for the next Falcon 1. Hawthorne built the rocket. The new engine was the configuration of that rocket. A test site can arrive in a later version. This version does not add one.

A click on a fleet row opens the rocket. The column stays put. The view is a dark sheet over the map. The rocket stands in the middle. Thrust, impulse, mass, and restarts sit around it. The name and the place sit in the top line: at the factory, in the bay, on the road, or on the pad.

Raise Merlin thrust moves here, off the Starfactory card. The button stays quiet until you ask to build this view. It spends the same 36 steel and takes the same 2 days. Thrust on that rocket rises from 340 kN to 381 kN. Later upgrades use the same places around the rocket. They do not get a new button on the factory.

Goals

Roadmap row 0.3.0-range is the same game, easier to read. The header, the build column, the inspector, the fleet, the goals, the log, and the fire close-up change how they look. They do not change what they do.

Today the chrome is a wood-panel RTS. The bar is .resource-bar. Side columns are .ui-panel on --panel #2a2218. Labels are tiny amber uppercase stacks. Buttons are brown .ui-btn. The map canvas sits on #2d4a28. That green site stays.

SpaceX feel belongs in the type, the header, the cards, the buttons, and the fire close-up. It does not replace the site with the dark three-column dashboard from ~/starship-dev. The roadmap already keeps that dashboard out.

This pass does not add a building, a resource, a rocket, or a fire rule. Merlin thrust, the pad check, the sky, steel, and propellant stay the numbers they are. The regions stay the same: top bar, build column, map, inspector, fleet, goals, log, and #event-stage.

Non-goals are a new mechanic, a split of index.html, and generated art. The winner is Option D. The top bar is larger than the Option A numerals. Thrust on the vehicle card keeps the Option A size. The lamp, the red edge, and the short limiter word stay.

The references are public write-ups of real SpaceX screens, not copies of their art. Shane Mielke’s webcast packages (2019 and the 2025 vehicle-wide V2) use a thin black strip, big numbers, and widgets that change with the vehicle. Eric Daly’s 2017 launch graphics are a small telemetry set plus a mission timeline. The GESAB console for SpaceX is black structure with red status light. The Starship HLS panel photo shows a right-hand engine and vehicle block beside a picture of the ship. This game keeps the green map in the middle and borrows only the chrome.

What stays

The rules stay. TUNING, ROCKET_DEFS, fireCheck, static-fire phases, refurb days, scrap, and the sky from the day number do not move. Buildings stay Starfactory, Mega Bay, and Orbital Pad. Resources stay steel and propellant. Falcon 1 stays the only rocket you can build.

Map colors stay. The site is the earthy green field. Drag and zoom stay on #map, with the same min and max. Esc still cancels a placement. Mute stays on #btn-mute, on the right of the header. The version label reads 0.3.0-range on the footer hint. The game you play is that screen.

No new data leaves the browser. The pass does not add an account, a network call, or a save. Tailwind stays the CDN script already in the file. New files stay out. The game stays one index.html.

Choice

You chose Option D. The screen stays the black range console. The lamp, the red edge, and the limiter word stay as written under that option. Options A, B, C, and E are not built.

The slice is 0.3.0-range. The old name, desk, is dropped. Range is the word on the lamp. That label sits on the footer hint in index.html.

The top bar is the large type on this screen. “Range open” or “Range closed” is about 22px, on the left, with the wind under it at about 15px. Steel, propellant, fleet, and day are about 40px in the center, with a short label above each number. That is bigger than Option A, whose header numerals are 28px. The sound button sits alone on the right.

On the vehicle card, thrust in kN is the large number from Option A. The first line of that card is still the limiter word from Option D. Impulse, mass, and restarts stay smaller under the thrust figure. Fleet, goals, the log, and the build column stay the Option D size.

Key decisions

  • Option D is the look. The code follows that choice when you ask for the restyle.
  • The top bar is larger than Option A: range sentence about 22px, wind about 15px, steel, propellant, and day about 40px. Thrust on the vehicle card stays at the Option A size.
  • No new rule. Same buildings, same Merlin fire, same sky, same resources.
  • The map stays green. SpaceX feel stays in the chrome around it.
