How a result is decided

Every part has a spec. Every flight has a requirement. You can read both before you commit the vehicle. The flight does what those numbers say. If the stack clears the bar, it flies. If it misses, the debrief names the short part: engine, structure, pad, a tank, or the sky. Wear climbs by a known amount on each firing and falls when the bay finishes a refurb.

A static fire is a hold-down test. Thrust, the pad, the tanks, and the wind decide it. Specific impulse and mass stay on the card until a hop, because impulse is how far a kilogram of propellant pushes you and mass is what the payload sum uses. The factory, the bay, the road, and the refurb stay as they are. New detail is a number on that loop, not a second game.

The first finished game is one rocket on the Texas site. Falcon 9, a second pad, Heavy, customers beyond one small commercial flight, Starlink, Starship, Louisiana, and xAI are the next patches. The yard, the longer flight cards, the Moon and Mars sites, Florida, and the picture and save work are phases after those. Each row is one addition to a game you can already finish. The story a player reads is the README. This page is the build map.

Already playable

You can build Falcon 1 only. The factory shows a Merlin card, and one upgrade raises its thrust. A static fire follows the numbers and the sky. The exact figures live in the game and in the field guide, not on this map.

Fuel

Falcon 1 flies on RP-1 and liquid oxygen. Each gets a tank and a price when that slice arrives. Methane arrives with Starship and reuses the oxygen tanks. What the fluids are is in the field guide.

Falcon 1, done

You are on the Texas dirt. You place the Starfactory, the Mega Bay, one Orbital Pad, an RP-1 tank, and a liquid-oxygen tank. The factory builds only Falcon 1 and holds it until the bay takes it. The bay houses the core and refurbs it. The pad lights it and then sends it home.

The engine lab shows thrust, specific impulse, mass, and restarts for the one Merlin. A purchase moves one number and applies to every Falcon 1 you already have. The structure lab shows dry mass and strength. The rocket panel sums them into payload. The pad can be strengthened. The sky stays in the header, with the next two days visible, and a closed range still refuses the fire. Cadence on screen is fires in the last 30 days and days since the last fire.

A hop reports altitude and speed from thrust, mass, and the load. An orbit attempt adds the upper-stage burn and leaves an orbit card: altitude, inclination, payload mass, and days of life. One commercial contract pays when that card matches the sale. Expend the core and the payload rises. Land it and the bay spends the printed refurb days.

Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, and Starship stay locked names.

Version draft

The label is N.x.y-codename. The first number is the chapter. Zero is Falcon 1 in Texas. Later chapters are the patches. The middle number is one playable slice. The last number is a small fix on that slice, and we do not spend it in advance. A version ships when you can see the change in the browser. If a row feels wrong after a sitting, we edit this table before we write the code.

The next slice is the screen you already have. The panels, the type, and the way a fire is explained need a pass before any new mechanic. That pass does not add a lab or a tank.

One slice at a time, in the order of that table. Play it. Change the row if the sitting feels wrong. Falcon 1 is done when you can play from an empty pad through a landed core and a paid orbit without needing another rocket. Pictures stay small until the fuel and the fire are readable.

Patches

  1. Falcon 9. Nine Merlins, more RP-1, more liquid oxygen, a pad that must already be strong. Same hop, orbit, and contract path.
  2. Second pad, then Falcon Heavy. The second pad is the same object on a second Texas footprint. Heavy is three cores, more of both fluids, and a longer pad recovery.
  3. Customers. NASA cargo, crew, other countries, and military cards. Each opens after you have flown that class.
  4. Starlink. Birds with mass, power, radio, and life. They use the same steel, tanks, and pads as a customer flight.
  5. Starship. Raptor, a methane tank and price, methalox loads on the oxygen you already store, a tower, a catch, and an orbit depot. Moon and Mars are contracts on that depot.
  6. Louisiana Starbase. A new site with the same buildings and the same fluids, empty at first. Texas sends a rocket there in a counted number of days. This is a site upgrade.
  7. xAI. A data center competes with the pads for steel and power. The panel shows power used and whether a cluster delayed a booster.

Later phases

Phases 0 through 5 are the draft you already had. Phases 6 through 10 are the future-feature notes, now versioned, and they stay below 5.0.0-compute. A new idea is a new row under phase 10, or a new phase 11, until you move it.

An older local prototype at ~/starship-dev/index.html already played a longer loop. The ladder, the landing choice, and the flight you watch come from that loop. Its dark dashboard stays out. Its coin flip at liftoff stays out.

  1. 6 · Yard. Land beside the pad, a workforce count, houses for the shift, then one-time payments for the first hop, the first orbit, the first landing, and a long run of recoveries.
  2. 7 · Longer flights. Cards beyond the one Falcon 1 sale. Expend, legs, or catch. A heatshield works and a flight-computer works. The full watch from preflight through recovery.
  3. 8 · Outer sites. A Moon site, then a Mars site. Texas still builds the rockets and fills the tanks.
  4. 9 · Florida. A third site, after Louisiana is fun. Same buildings, same fluids, a counted transfer.
  5. 10 · Picture and record. Shapes that change with level, plumes, lesson pictures, sound, save slots, a flight table, and a harder or easier start.
