How a result is decided

Every part has a spec. Every flight has a requirement. You can read both before you commit the vehicle. The flight does what those numbers say. If the stack clears the bar, it flies. If it misses, the debrief names the short part: engine, structure, propellant, or pad. Wear climbs by a known amount on each firing and falls when the bay finishes a refurb. A tired core is worse by that amount on the next flight.

Margin is the gap between what you have and what the mission asks. A large margin is a clean flight. A thin margin still flies and costs more wear. A negative margin fails on the named part. There is no separate luck roll.

You seeIt means
Thrust, impulse, mass, restartsThe engine, on the stand and in the burn.
Dry mass, strength, max-qThe structure. Too weak for the trajectory and the stage fails there.

The desk you always have

Rockets are individual articles. Each one is in the bay, on the road, on a pad, or on an orbit card, with its own condition and its own parts. Pads are a queue: one vehicle on the mount, a recovery counted in days after the fire, propellant drawn from the tank. The factory holds one finished article until the bay takes it, so a full bay stops the line. Steel and propellant are the two stocks. Labs spend steel and days to move one spec. Contracts spend a vehicle, a pad slot, and propellant, and pay steel when the delivered orbit matches what was sold.

The map stays the Texas dirt you already play until a later era adds another shore. New capability arrives as a lab, a tier on a building you already placed, or a card for something already in the air. A second pad is the same pad object on a second footprint.

A lab is a bench inside a building you already have, or one new building when the bench no longer fits. Each purchase moves one number and changes the silhouette of that building by a small, visible amount.

Era 1 · Parts on the stand

First slice. The rockets already in the game grow a readable spec. Falcon 1 is one engine and a short stack. Falcon 9 is nine engines of that same family and a heavier stage. Static fire still happens on the pad you have. The close-up uses the spec: one bell or nine, thrust and impulse printed beside the flame.

Engine tier 2 spends steel and days and raises thrust and impulse by a small printed percent, and cuts wear one step. Tier 3 raises them again. The panel shows the tier on every vehicle of that family already in the fleet. A fire with a negative margin fails the engine or the structure, whichever number was short, and the bay either scraps the article or starts the long refurb.

Done when you can open the factory, read thrust, impulse, mass, and restarts, improve one of them, static-fire a Falcon 9 on a level-2 pad, and read a margin line that matches the arithmetic.

Era 2 · Hop and orbit

The same vehicle leaves the stand. A hop spends the first-stage burn and stops at altitude and speed computed from thrust, mass, and propellant. An orbit attempt adds the upper stage: vacuum impulse, a restart, and a guidance number from the avionics lab. The orbit card keeps altitude, inclination, payload mass aboard, power, and days of life. Several cards can be open at once. A missed orbit names the short number and removes that card.

Payload the stack can lift becomes a real figure here, in kilograms, from public Falcon-class targets written into the slice: a few hundred kilograms for the small rocket, on the order of ten tonnes and later about twenty tonnes for Falcon 9 as the tanks and engines tier up. The exact kilograms are locked in the slice, then adjusted if a sitting feels wrong.

Era 3 · Customers

A contract appears only after you have already done that class of flight. The card shows orbit, mass, pad days, propellant, and steel paid on delivery. You accept it by assigning one rocket and one pad. Delivery is the orbit card matching the sold orbit and mass.

NASA cargo, stationA streak of clean orbits and a payload in the cargo-capsule classPressurized cargo. Occupies the pad longer. Pays more steel.
CrewA longer clean streak and a capsule you have flownStation crew. A thin margin is a failed acceptance, not a flown risk you discover later.

Era 4 · Fly the core again

Recovery is a spec. Legs, grid fins, and the propellant you hold back all have mass, and that mass comes out of payload. Expend the stage and the payload figure rises and the bay must build a new core. Land it and the bay spends the printed refurb days, and the next flight starts from the condition you left. The pad, or a drone ship counted as a slow pad at sea, must be free and strong enough for the landing load.

Falcon Heavy is three cores you already know how to build, stacked as one article. It needs pad level 3, more steel, more propellant, and a longer pad recovery. Side boosters use the recovery spec. The center core is the one that usually comes home more tired.

Era 5 · Starlink

When a small satellite can ride a repeatable flight, you may keep the slot. Each bird shows mass, power, radio, and design life. The constellation panel shows planes, healthy birds, and how much of the Earth they cover. Coverage and spare birds are what let you sell service later. Building birds uses steel, so the hall competes with customer rockets for the factory, the pads, and the tanks. That squeeze is the point of the era.

A useful first target is a few dozen birds in one shell before the panel claims real coverage. Mass on the order of a few hundred kilograms per bird, life counted in years, power and radio printed. The slice locks the kilograms.

Era 6 · Starship

Raptor is a second engine family in the same lab: chamber pressure, thrust, mass, flights before a rebuild. The ship and the booster are a structure tier: tank mass, heat-shield health, flap strength, catch load. The Texas pad grows a tower. Catch is the recovery spec for this stack. A tanker is a ship whose payload is propellant delivered to a depot card in orbit. A ship on orbit shows its tanks, shield health, and whether the next reentry is inside the shield number.

A Moon landing and a Mars window are contracts that draw from that depot. Until Starship’s own cadence is real, Falcon pads and the Merlin line still pay the steel. Merlin does not leave the game when Raptor arrives.

Era 7 · Company beside the rockets

Starlink service pays steel and a power budget. When the constellation is up and the launch rhythm holds, you can buy xAI. A data center is a site that wants megawatts, chips, and the same attention as a pad. The panel shows power used and training runs finished, and whether a new cluster delayed a booster. A second shore, Louisiana first, then a Florida pad, is another copy of factory, bay, and pad, fed by transfer days from Texas. The Gulf map opens only after that second site works.
