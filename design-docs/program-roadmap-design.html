How a result is decided

Every part has a spec. Every flight has a requirement. You can read both before you commit the vehicle. The flight does what those numbers say. If the stack clears the bar, it flies. If it misses, the debrief names the short part: engine, structure, fuel lines, pad, a tank, or the sky. Life falls by a known amount on each firing and comes back when the bay finishes the refurbishment.

A static fire is a hold-down test. Thrust, the pad, the tanks, and the wind decide it. Specific impulse and engine mass stay on the card until a flight. Specific impulse is how much push one kilogram of propellant gives. Payload capacity uses engine mass, stage dry mass, and specific impulse. The factory, the bay, the road, and the refurb stay as they are. New detail is a number on that loop, not a second game.

Phase 0 is Starship development at Starbase in Texas, up to orbit. You build a development article, test it, hop it, stack it, and fly it until it makes orbit. After orbit you choose where to go next. Starlink, the Moon and Mars, and the Falcon story at the real Falcon sites are the three branches. Each row is one addition to a game you can already finish. The story a player reads is the README. This page is the build map.

Already playable

You can build one rocket today. The factory shows a test article, and Raise Raptor thrust takes it from Raptor 1 to Raptor 2. A static fire follows the numbers and the sky. The exact figures live in the game and in the field guide, not on this map.

The shipped slices, 0.1.0-foundation through 0.11.0-screen, were built on a Falcon 1 stand-in on the Texas site. The yard, the map, Land, the Inspector, the purse, the tanks, the motion, and the part sheet all carry over. 0.12.0-raptor makes the vehicle a Starship development article.

Fuel

Starship flies on liquid methane and liquid oxygen. In phase 0 methane replaces RP-1, and the oxygen tanks stay. RP-1 comes back with the Falcon story in phase 4. What the fluids are is in the field guide.

Phase 0, done

You are on the Texas dirt at Starbase. You place the Starfactory, the Mega Bay, one Orbital Pad, a methane tank, and a liquid-oxygen tank. The factory builds a Starship development article and holds it until the bay takes it. The bay houses it and refurbishes it. The pad tests it, lights it, and then sends it home.

The engine lab shows thrust, specific impulse, engine mass, and restarts for Raptor. The structure lab shows stage dry mass and the structural limit. The rocket sheet still paints Structure, Fuel lines, and Engine. A prototype can carry a hidden defect. A cryo proof, a tanking run, and a static fire can each find one. You can skip a test, and the flight then carries that risk.

The vehicle grows one step at a time. First a hop with one Raptor. Then a high hop with a flip and a landing. Then a booster and a ship stacked at the tower. Then a suborbital flight test, where staging can fail. Then orbit. Hops land. Flight tests in phase 0 do not come home, and the booster and the ship are lost at sea.

A ship that makes orbit leaves an orbit card: altitude, inclination, payload mass, and orbital life in days. The first paying contract pays when a flight meets the sale. That orbit closes phase 0. Recovery and the catch are phase 1.

Version draft

The label is N.x.y-codename. The first number is the chapter. Zero is Starship development at Starbase in Texas, up to orbit. Later chapters are the patches. The middle number is one playable slice. The last number is a small fix on that slice, and we do not spend it in advance. A version ships when you can see the change in the browser. If a row feels wrong after a sitting, we edit this table before we write the code. We restructured this map on 2026-11-09, so phase 0 is Starship and the Falcon story became a later branch.

The live release is 0.12.0-raptor. The factory builds a test article with one Raptor. The header counts methane and liquid oxygen. The rocket sheet paints Structure, Fuel lines, and Engine (Raptor). The hop comes later. The next row is 0.13.0-proof. Then come the prototype tests, defects, the hop, the high hop, the stack, the suborbital test, and orbit. Phase 0 rules are done at 0.19.0-orbit. Tracking, the building pictures, and the final motion pass come after orbit, still in phase 0.

Choose a branch

After orbit, phase 1 comes first. It brings the booster home, adds the catch and the refurb, and speeds up the factory. Then you choose a branch. Phases 2, 3, and 4 are that choice. You can play them in any order after phase 1.

Starlink puts your own satellites up. The Moon and Mars start with a depot in orbit, and they need the catch. The Falcon story goes back to where SpaceX began, at the real Falcon sites. Starbase in Texas is the Starship site. Falcon never started there.

Patches

  1. Recovery and catch. Bring the booster home or fly it expendable. The tower catches it. The Mega Bay checks a flown stage for defects and refurbishes it. Then the Starfactory builds faster.
  2. Starlink. Satellites with mass, power, radio, and life. They use the same steel, tanks, and pads as a customer flight.
  3. Moon and Mars. A depot in orbit, then a Moon site, then a Mars site. Texas still builds the rockets and fills the tanks.
  4. Falcon history. The rockets are built in California. Falcon 1 flies from Kwajalein. Falcon 9 opens at Cape Canaveral SLC-40, then Vandenberg, then the 39A lease. Falcon Heavy and crew fly from 39A. RP-1 comes back here.
  5. xAI. A data center competes with the pads for steel and power. The panel shows power used and whether a cluster delayed a booster.

Later phases

Phases 1 through 5 are the patches above. Phases 6 through 9 are the future-feature notes, now versioned, and they stay below 5.0.0-compute. The old Louisiana and Florida Starbases are gone, because the real Falcon sites replace them. A newer idea is a new row under phase 9, or a new phase 10, until you move it.

An older local prototype at ~/starship-dev/index.html already played a longer loop. The ladder, the landing choice, and the flight you watch come from that loop. Its dark dashboard stays out. Its coin flip at liftoff stays out.

  1. 6 · Yard. Land beside the pad, a workforce count, houses for the shift, a test stand, a tank farm, and one-time payments for the first hop, the first orbit, the first catch, and a long run of recoveries.
  2. 7 · Longer flights. Cards beyond the first contract. Expend, legs, or catch. A heatshield works and a flight-computer works. The full watch from preflight through recovery.
  3. 8 · Picture and record. Shapes that change with level, plumes, lesson pictures, sound, save slots, a flight table, and a harder or easier start.
  4. 9 · Fleet desk. One shared desk to watch many rockets and sites at once, once the game has that many.
