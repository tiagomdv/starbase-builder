What you see

You finish the cryo proof, the tanking run, and a clean static fire. The data review takes 1 day. The pad then says the rocket is ready. The line that said the hop comes later is gone.

You pick a height in the pinned actions. The chips are 25 m, 50 m, and 150 m. One fact line under them names the load. The parts table moves its Needs cells to that burn and that landing floor.

The tanks load on their own clock. The day holds. The camera then locks a frame about 400 m wide. A countdown runs from T-10 on the screen. There is no sound. The hopper rises, holds the height, and comes down on a mark about 100 m east of the mount.

A soft landing rolls back to the mount. That roll is a road of 0.5 to 1 day. An inspection then takes 1 day on the mount. A hard landing names the short part. You press Return to Mega Bay. A scrub leaves the rocket ready and spends nothing.

The same rocket can hop again when the inspection is done, the lines and the engine still have the seconds, the tanks hold the load, and Structure is still at least 70.

The flight

The hop uses the Raptor you already have. Full thrust is 1814 kN. Specific impulse is 330 seconds. Gravity is FLIGHT_G, which is 9.81. The hidden launch stays hidden. A hop does not lose the rocket at sea.

The burn holds the weight. Hover throttle is the weight divided by 1814 kN. It is 56.5 percent at 25 m, 57.6 percent at 50 m, and 59.1 percent at 150 m. The flame length follows the thrust the engine is using. A 1400 kN engine at full thrust draws a shorter flame than a 1814 kN engine at full thrust.

Propellant for a full burn follows from that hover. Flow at hover is the flight mass divided by 330. The load below is that flow for the whole burn, split by the 3.6 mix, and rounded to the nearest kg.

const HOP_DRY_KG = 102000;
const HOP_ISP_S = 330;
const HOP_LAND_TWR = 1.45;
const HOP_G = 9.81;

function hopProp(burnS) {
  const x = burnS / HOP_ISP_S;
  const prop = HOP_DRY_KG * x / (1 - x);
  const ch4 = Math.round(prop / 4.6);
  const lox = Math.round(ch4 * 3.6);
  return { ch4, lox, flightKg: HOP_DRY_KG + ch4 + lox };
}
function hopFloorKn(flightKg) {
  return Math.round(flightKg * HOP_G * HOP_LAND_TWR / 1000);
}

The landing

The landing reads the thrust, the fuel lines, the engine seconds, and the pad. The same numbers always end the same way. A closed range or a short tank still scrubs. The rocket stays ready. No propellant leaves. No life is spent. Nothing is spent.

A short pad lights. The log names the pad. Structure loses 40 and stays at 20 or more. The rocket needs Mega Bay. It is not scrap. It is a miss, the same as a short pad on a static fire. Raptor 2 is 2256 kN. Pad level 1 holds 2000 kN, so this case is reachable without a test hook. The proof and the tanking run clear.

The floor on the parts table is the soft-landing line. A landing is soft when the thrust is at least that floor. A healthy Raptor at 1814 kN clears every floor. Thrust at 1400 kN clears none of them. It also fails the static fire floor of 1500 kN, so the player meets that case through setThrust. The log names the engine and prints both numbers.

The tooth in a normal game is the fuel lines. Healthy lines are 40 seconds. One 150 m hop leaves 18 seconds. The next 150 m burn needs 22. The Margin bar shows 18 against 22 before the press. Hop stays on. The burn lasts the seconds the lines or the engine still have. A short burn lands hard.

Under 2 seconds of burn, the rocket does not leave the mount. The engine did light. Structure loses 40 and stays at 20 or more. The rocket needs Mega Bay. It is not scrap.

If it leaves and the burn ends early, or the thrust is under the floor, the landing is hard. Structure loses 40 and stays at 20 or more. The rocket needs Mega Bay. It is not scrap. The log names every short part. A clean hop cuts Structure by 4, 6, or 10, from the table.

Propellant used is the load times the seconds burned, divided by the full burn, rounded to the nearest kg. The unused share goes back to the pad tanks. A full burn uses the whole load. A second 150 m hop that burns 18 seconds uses 1296 kg of methane and 4665 kg of oxygen. The other 288 kg and 1037 kg go back.

Engine seconds and line seconds fall by the seconds burned. A fire still spends 5 engine seconds and does not spend the lines. The hop does not call afterFireLives.

A hard landing clears the cryo proof and the tanking run, the same way a lit miss does. A soft landing keeps them. Structure under 70 blocks the button. The line names Structure, the number it has, and 70. That press does not start a fill.

An engine with 0 seconds does not light. The pad names the engine. The rocket stays as it is.

Skip in the middle of that road finishes the road, then jumps. The days are paid. The arrival matches a waited road within 0.05 day. A hard landing does not start the roll.

After the inspection the rocket is ready again. You pick the next height. The parts table uses the life, the tanks, and the thrust that are left. After a hard landing you press Return to Mega Bay and use the bay jobs you already have.

The review and the goal

Every 30 days the program review pays 12 money for each clean test in that window. The review runs once inside the day change, when the new day is 30, 60, or 90. setDay does not pay. A later frame on that same day does not pay again.

The window is the clean rows whose day is after lastReviewDay and on or before the review day. A clean test is a cryo proof that held, a tanking run that held, a static fire that held, or a hop that landed soft. A skip, a scrub, a miss, and a hard landing pay nothing. The log says how much the review added. A quiet window says that no clean test is in it.

The goals sheet in index.html is a fixed list of seven. Those seven pays sum to 360. The sheet gains a row, "Land a hop.", for 60. That 60 sits level with the static fire goal. The pay never shows on the sheet unless that row is added. The first soft landing pays it once. A hard landing does not pay it. The seven goals stay. 6.1.0-rounds rebalances the review later.

A proof, a tanking run, a static fire, or a hop writes result clean or miss when it ends. A static fire today is stored in fireDays, not in testDays. It also writes a testDays row with that result, so the review can see it. fireDays stays for the cadence count. A failed proof today writes kind: "proof" with no pass flag. This row adds the result. A skip stays unpaid. A scrub writes no clean row. A row with no result pays nothing, including every row already in an old save.

The pad record can add a hop count for the last 30 days. The header cadence number still waits for 0.18.1-pipeline.

Real life

Starhopper flew at Boca Chica on one Raptor. Wikipedia's list of Starship flight tests records two tethered hops. On 3 April 2019 it rose under 0.3 m for about 3 seconds. On 5 April 2019 it rose about 1 m for about 5 seconds and hit the tether limits.

On 25 July 2019 it flew free for about 22 seconds and reached about 20 m. That same list calls the height 20 m. NASASpaceflight's 27 August account calls the July hop about 18 m. The 25 m button and the 50 m button are game choices.

On 27 August 2019 Starhopper flew to 150 m in 57 seconds and landed about 100 m from the launch pad. The flight list gives the height and the 57 seconds. The vehicle list gives the distance. Scientific American describes the separate landing pad and the 57 seconds. An attempt on 26 August shut down at ignition. The plan had been near 200 m. The FAA ceiling for the flight was 150 m. NASASpaceflight wrote that up on 27 August 2019.

The game burn stays 22 seconds. A 57 second burn at this dry mass asks for 4630 kg of methane and 16668 kg of oxygen. The level 2 tanks hold 4029 kg and 14502 kg, so that load does not fit. The real 57 seconds stay in this section.

SN5 hopped 150 m on 4 August 2020. The flight list times that hop at about 51 seconds. SN6 hopped 150 m on 3 September 2020 and landed on the pad beside the stand. Each of those flights used one Raptor. Everyday Astronaut's SN6 page is the plan for that hop.
