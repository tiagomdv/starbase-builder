Road time and the day clock

Today the road, the lift, and the return run on real seconds. A day runs on its own clock. A day is 4 s at 1x and 0.5 s in ?test, divided by the speed. So the same road costs a different number of days at each speed. The longest road is 14 s. That is 3.5 days at 1x, 14 days at 4x, and 28 days in ?test.

After, the road and the lift are measured in game days. They move with the day clock. The same road costs the same days at 1x, at 3x, and in ?test. Fires, flights, and the tank fill keep their real seconds, and the day holds while they run.

These numbers are locked. A road takes its length over 728 px per day, held between half a day and 1 day. The return road uses 784 px per day, which keeps today's ratio of 56 to 52. Raise and lower take half a day each. At 1x that is 2 to 4 s of road and a 2 s lift, close to today. At 3x it is 0.7 to 1.3 s of road.

// TUNING: the road and the lift follow the day clock, not real seconds.
ROLLOUT_PX_PER_DAY: 728, RETURN_PX_PER_DAY: 784,
ROLLOUT_MIN_DAYS: 0.5, ROLLOUT_MAX_DAYS: 1, LIFT_DAYS: 0.5,

// beginRoad: duration is in days.
const duration = clamp(metrics.totalLen / pxPerDay, TUNING.ROLLOUT_MIN_DAYS, TUNING.ROLLOUT_MAX_DAYS);

// updateRollout: a road or a lift moves by the share of a day this frame spent.
r.t += (dt * 1000) / dayMs();

The log and the Inspector say days. "Starhopper 1 is rolling to the Orbital Pad · 0.6 days left." The wiki rollout fact says "half a day to 1 day". It adds one sentence: the road length is a game number, and a real rollout takes a few hours.

Skip during a road finishes the road, and any lift that is running or that the road starts, in that order. Then it jumps to the next event as it does today. The days still pay for the trip. Skip adds the trip's remaining days to the clock before the jump, so the rocket lands on the same day it would have reached by waiting.

A save written before this row holds a road in seconds. On load, the game keeps the share of the trip already driven, t / duration. It recomputes the full trip in days from the stored road length, or half a day for a lift, and sets t to that same share of the new trip. A road saved halfway arrives after half of the new trip. A new save marks each road with unit: "day". The save stays version 1.

The day holds

Today the day keeps moving during a fill, a fire, and a flight. 0.14.2-seed kept that rule. This row replaces it. The day holds while a fill, a fire, or a flight runs.

You see the day number stop. The map job note reads "The day holds while the tanks fill", "The day holds while the engine burns", or "The day holds during the flight". When the motion ends, the note goes and the day moves again from the same point. The top bar does not change.

Holding stops everything that runs on the day clock, on purpose. The share of the current day, G.dayAcc, stays where it is. The factory build and the bay job countdowns stop. A road or a lift elsewhere in the yard stops too, because it now runs on days. The fill, the fire, and the flight themselves keep their real seconds. When 0.18.1-pipeline lets the factory build during a test, that build pauses during a fire too.

// frame(): the day clock holds while a fill, a fire, or a flight runs.
const hold = motionKind("fill") || motionKind("fire") || motionKind("fly");
if (!G.paused && !hold) {
  G.dayAcc += dt * 1000;
  // dayTick() as today
}
if (!G.paused) updateRollout(dt); // roads and lifts read dayMs() and skip their step while hold is true

Pause still holds both clocks. Pause during a fire freezes the fire and the day. During a fill, a fire, or a flight, Skip does not jump. It logs the same line it logs today. The save does not change. A fill, a fire, and a flight are not saved today, so a load never starts inside a hold.

Starhopper

The first Raptor vehicle at Boca Chica was Starhopper. Its first static fire was in April 2019. So every build is named Starhopper and a number: Starhopper 1, Starhopper 2, and so on. There is no short serial. SH stays free for Super Heavy at 0.20.0-stack. SN serials start at 0.19.0-high-hop. "Test article" and TA leave the screen, the log, and the wiki. Grasshopper stays out of phase 0. It was a Falcon 9 hopper at McGregor, and it belongs to the Falcon story.

The rocket key in the code stays article. That keeps the defect order, the seed salts, builtArticles, and articleN exactly as they are. The rocket's name becomes "Starhopper". The new vehicle's name is the rocket name, a space, and the number, in place of `${r.short}-${n}`. The number rule stays: the live count of that rocket plus 1.

Each vehicle also has a numeric id. Clicks, the save, and the rocket window use that id. The display name is only a label. The test hooks __sb.startRoll(name, dest), __sb.stage(name), and __sb.startBayCheck(name) find the vehicle by its display name. After this row they take "Starhopper 1", not "TA-1".

Old saves are renamed on load, in applySave:

  • Each fleet name has /\bTA-(\d+)\b/g replaced with "Starhopper $1". TA-1 loads as Starhopper 1.
  • The fleet loader's default name changes from "TA" to "Starhopper". vehicleSave writes the name as it is, so the next save holds the new names.
  • Each of the 45 saved log rows gets the same whole-word swap. "Test article" becomes "Starhopper" in those rows too, so the log and the fleet agree.

The save stays version 1. No other field changes. Text that changes with the name: the Build button reads "Build Starhopper", the Starfactory owned line reads "Builds a Starhopper", and the wiki lines that say "a test article" name Starhopper. "A test article carries no payload" becomes "Starhopper carries no payload" on its wiki page. The 0.18.0-hop row text is updated to match.
